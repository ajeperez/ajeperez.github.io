Discrete φⁿ frequencies in spontaneous neural oscillations

A precision measurement program — and a broader theoretical framework

Alexandre Joseph Etienne PEREZ  ·  Lausanne, May 14, 2026  ·  ajeprz@proton.me

Across seven independent LFP datasets covering two species (Homo sapiens, Mus musculus) and three preparation types (cortical organoids, brain organoids, ex vivo neonatal cortical slices), the dominant peak frequencies of spontaneous neural oscillations preferentially align on the discrete grid fₙ = f₀ · φⁿ at sub-1% relative precision. The signal reproduces between platforms to 78 mHz (p = 0.922), is stable over multi-week timescales (CV = 0.3% over 17 days on one human organoid), and is recovered by an independent DeepBreath-style analysis as a globally sustained — not event-locked — spectral feature.

This empirical finding is reported as a stand-alone preprint, prepared for external scientific submission and independent replication. Possible biophysical mechanisms (φ as the most irrational ratio of dynamical systems, KAM theory, phyllotaxis-like packing) are discussed without commitment. The motivation for testing the discrete φⁿ hypothesis is grounded in a broader theoretical framework — developed separately by the author and listed below — but the empirical work does not depend on that framework.

Discrete frequency convergence in spontaneous oscillations of organized neural networks

Quantitative evidence for fₙ = f₀·φⁿ at sub-1% relative precision in cortical organoids and ex vivo cortical slices

A precision measurement across seven LFP datasets, two species and three preparation types. Best relative error 0.0% on φ⁶ and φ⁸; inter-platform reproducibility at 78 mHz (p = 0.922); longitudinal stability CV = 0.3% over 17 days; χ² test of concentration on φⁿ across all preparations (N = 3,275): χ² = 2,475.61, p < 0.001. Independent validation by a DeepBreath-style spectro-temporal pipeline (Heitmann et al. 2023, npj Digital Medicine — author co-authored). All raw data publicly available (DANDI, Zenodo).
